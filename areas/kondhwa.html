Interior Designers in Kondhwa, Pune

Kondhwa's appeal is space. The apartments here run larger than comparable budgets buy elsewhere in Pune, which draws families who need room to spread out — often three generations, or two working adults with children and visiting parents.

Large homes are a zoning problem

A four-bedroom flat is not a three-bedroom flat with a room added. Past a certain size the design problem stops being how to fit things in and becomes how to separate them properly.

The questions that matter are which rooms need to be quiet and which are shared, whether guests reach the living area without passing bedroom doors, and where the household actually collects at the end of a day. Get the zoning right and a large home feels calm; get it wrong and it feels like a corridor with rooms off it.

Giving each room character without fragmenting the home

Four bedrooms is more than enough for a home to feel disjointed. The way through is one material language repeated with variation, not four separate schemes negotiated room by room.

In Sachin Sharma's 4 BHK at Escon the dining sits under a slatted timber ceiling that defines the zone without a wall — which is exactly the problem timber slats solve well in an open plan. They add warmth to a room that would otherwise be all flat surfaces, and they break up sound in a way plain gypsum does not.

Ceilings and lighting in bigger volumes

Larger rooms make lighting harder, not easier. A single ambient source that works in a compact living room leaves a big one patchy and flat.

We plan three layers: ambient light to fill the room, task light aimed where something actually happens, and accent light to pick out texture at night. In a home this size, being able to run the cove alone in the evening without the downlights changes the feel of the space more than any additional fitting would. That means planning the switching before the wiring closes, not after.

Storage across a larger home

Bigger homes accumulate more, and the storage has to be planned as a system rather than room by room. Ceiling-height wardrobes in every bedroom, a full-height utility unit near the kitchen, a genuine place for luggage, and a drop at the entry so the foyer stays clear. Without that, a four-bedroom flat quietly turns one bedroom into a store room.
