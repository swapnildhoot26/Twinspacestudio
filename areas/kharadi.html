Interior Designers in Kharadi, Pune

Kharadi is Pune's fastest-moving handover market. Towers around EON IT Park, World Trade Center and the Nagar Road corridor hand over in blocks, and hundreds of families take possession of a bare flat within weeks of each other. This page covers what that means practically, what a full interior costs, how we compress the timeline without cutting the joinery, and a 3 BHK we delivered here.

What Kharadi homes are like

Almost everything is post-2015 high-rise. Flats are typically 2 and 3 BHK between 700 and 1,250 square feet of carpet area, in towers of fifteen floors and upward, with two or three lifts and a properly planned service core. Walls are square, ceilings are honest, and the plans repeat floor to floor.

Owners are overwhelmingly technology and consulting professionals working within three kilometres of home, many of them relocating from Bangalore, Hyderabad or overseas. A high proportion buy off-plan and see the finished flat for the first time at possession, which shapes the brief: the reference images are already collected, and the questions are about cost, sequence and accountability.

Handover to move-in, and why the clock matters

The defining Kharadi constraint is that people are usually paying rent and a home loan at the same time. Every week of fit-out has a real number attached to it, so the timeline is not a preference — it is a line in the household budget.

Ten to twelve weeks from approved drawings is our standard programme, and in Kharadi the way to protect it is to do the slow work before possession. Drawings can be finalised off the builder's plan and a site measurement taken on handover day. Material can be selected and ordered while the flat is still being snagged. Society registration and the service lift booking can be started the moment the allotment letter arrives.

Done that way, week one is carpentry rather than paperwork. Done the other way — starting the conversation after you have the keys — the same project finishes four to five weeks later for no design reason at all.

Snagging the flat before you start

New does not mean finished. Before any of our work begins we walk the flat against a snag list and hand it to the builder: level of the floor, fall in the bathrooms and utility, window and door alignment, seepage at external walls, socket count against the actual layout, and whether the promised electrical points exist where the brochure said.

Getting that resolved by the builder before joinery is fitted is worth real money. Once a wardrobe stands against a wall, a damp patch behind it becomes your problem rather than theirs.

What Kharadi briefs usually include

A full kitchen replacement is near-universal, because the builder platform is a counter and nothing else. A television wall with concealed wiring. Wardrobes taken to the slab. And, in almost every flat, a proper work setup — Kharadi has a higher proportion of hybrid workers than anywhere else we build, and two people on simultaneous calls in a 3 BHK is a planning problem worth solving on paper.

Balconies matter here more than most areas. The towers are high, the views are long, and a balcony at the fourteenth floor is genuinely usable if it is given decking, shade and light. It is one of the few places where spending on the outside of the flat repays daily.

Inside a Kharadi 3 BHK

In a 3 BHK in Kharadi we ran an arched motif from the foyer through to the guest bedroom so the flat reads as one designed home rather than a set of separately decorated rooms. The entry took fluted panelling and a low shoe cabinet; a black arched mirror sits over the console opposite.

The living room was built around a marble television wall that opens to the balcony. The kitchen took sage green base units under black wall cabinets, with a tall unit housing the fridge. The pooja niche was set into an arch with backlit lotus artwork and drawers beneath it, and the master bedroom picked the arch up again in a blush wardrobe.

What a Kharadi interior costs

A full 2 BHK runs ₹12–16 lakh and a full 3 BHK ₹16–21 lakh, itemised before anything is ordered. Kharadi projects tend to sit in the upper half of those bands, not because the area is charged differently but because bare handover means every line is new — there is no existing kitchen, wardrobe or ceiling to carry forward.

Two Kharadi-specific lines worth budgeting deliberately. Balcony treatment, typically ₹70,000 to ₹1.6 lakh depending on size and whether you glaze it. And air-conditioning coordination: high-rise flats need the outdoor unit positions agreed with the society, and the concealed pipe runs planned before the false ceiling closes.

Where the budget goes in a bare-handover flat

In a Kharadi 3 BHK, joinery is close to half the project — kitchen, wardrobes, television unit and entry storage. False ceilings and lighting take roughly a fifth, which is higher than in older areas because new towers have the height to allow a proper ceiling and owners generally want one. Electrical work is a real line rather than a nominal one, since builder point counts assume a far simpler home than anyone actually lives in.

The remainder covers painting, plumbing changes, hardware, loose furniture and supervision. Two quotes twenty per cent apart on the same drawing set almost always differ in the carcass material, the hinge and channel brands, and the edge banding rather than in anything visible on day one.

Because Kharadi flats are square and repeat floor to floor, there is little on-site adjustment and less waste than in an older building. That shows up as a tighter gap between the quoted figure and the final one.

How Kharadi compares with the areas around it

Buyers here are usually also considering Viman Nagar, Kalyani Nagar, Wagholi and Mundhwa, and the fit-out implications differ.

Against Viman Nagar, Kharadi towers are newer and taller, so lifts and service cores are better planned but the fit-out rush at handover is more intense. Against Kalyani Nagar, flats here are larger for the money and far more uniform, which makes the quote more predictable but leaves less character to work with. Against Mundhwa, Kharadi's societies are established and their rules are settled rather than being written as you go.

The practical read: Kharadi is the most schedule-sensitive area we work in, and the one where preparation before possession pays back most directly.

High-rise site logistics

Working on the eighteenth floor is not harder than the third, but it is less forgiving. Everything arrives by one service lift shared with every other flat under fit-out in the same tower, and a forgotten item is a forty-minute round trip rather than a walk to the van.

We plan deliveries as consolidated loads with a written list per trip, and keep a small stock of consumables in the flat. Societies here usually require the contractor to be registered with the facility management company, worker identity records on file, and debris bagged and removed on defined days.

Can you start work before I take possession?

We can do everything except build. Drawings can be finalised off the builder's plan, material selected and ordered, and society registration and the service-lift booking started as soon as the allotment letter arrives. That is what turns a fifteen-week project into a ten-week one.

How long does a full 3 BHK take in Kharadi?

Ten to twelve weeks from approved drawings to final styling. The variable in Kharadi is rarely the work itself; it is how much was arranged before the keys were collected.
